Moment of truth
The first patient arrives at 08:00. If the MFA has cleared the overnight backlog and verified the schedule, the day starts smoothly. Hardware failure or overwhelming to-dos means the MFA is already behind before door opens.
Registration is the highest-stakes MFA task. Every error — wrong Schein, unresolved cost carrier, missed enrollment — propagates through the billing chain and surfaces as a rejection weeks later.
Thorough intake (complete anamnesis, vitals, documents scanned, enrollment checked) saves the doctor 2–3 minutes per patient. A rushed intake creates downstream friction both pay for.
The MFA's ability to keep the practice flowing during peak hours is invisible — until it breaks down. The PVS should make this orchestration visible and manageable.
Prescriptions and forms are where small MFA errors have outsized consequences. The PVS should make the correct action easier than the incorrect one.
The MFA is the last line of defense for practice revenue. Every billing error that slips through means rejected claims, delayed reimbursement, and potential audit flags.
