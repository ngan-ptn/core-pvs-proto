Moment of truth
The first 5 minutes of the day set the doctor's confidence. A clear, dense overview with clinical context means the day starts controlled. A blank calendar with time slots means the doctor is already behind.
The handoff is the most fragile point. If everything is ready — Schein, anamnesis, relevant history surfaced — consultation starts immediately. If anything is missing, the doctor loses 1–2 min per patient, compounding to 30–60 min lost per day.
The quality of the clinical encounter depends on how little the PVS intrudes. The best experience: the doctor can focus entirely on the patient. The worst: more time navigating the PVS than listening.
ICD coding is the single highest-friction documentation task. If the PVS helps find the right code fast and explains validation errors in clinical language, documentation flows. Each patient adds 30–60 seconds of friction = 20–30 min lost per day.
Prescriptions and referrals are where clinical decision meets administrative execution. Every second of friction is time stolen from the next patient. Best: "one click to prescribe what I just decided." Worst: a 5-dialog journey with a physical signature.
End of day reveals the true cost of every friction point accumulated. A PVS that prevents administrative debt (continuous validation, smart defaults, auto-filled forms) gives the doctor their evening back. A deferral-first PVS creates burnout.
